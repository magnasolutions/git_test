### Brokerage · Abu Dhabi · Ranked 1st

**[INFERRED]**

  *Reasoning: publishing effort tracks commercial interest.* This is not evidence that PSI has ever
  closed a rent-to-own deal.

  have been individually sold. Ownership is therefore likely **fragmented across many private
  landlords**, not held by one developer. *Reasoning: 2,376 freehold units in a community completed
  years ago are not typically still on the developer's books.* If that is right, **no single
  developer can supply this product in the only price band where it works — only an aggregator can.**
  That makes a brokerage structurally necessary, not merely convenient.

### Why they might say yes
- **A yes costs them nothing.** No capital, no inventory, no product committee. They are being asked
  to try to originate a deal, and they are paid if it closes.
- **They have already staked a public position** that rent-to-own is a practical 2026 route to
  ownership [§1.4]. An approach asking them to make good on that is aligned with their own marketing,
  not opposed to it.
- **Differentiation.** The research's headline finding is that there is **zero verified, currently
  active villa/townhouse rent-to-own in Abu Dhabi at any price** [Headline]. A broker who can
  originate the first one owns an uncontested category.
- **The demand story is specific and verifiable to them.** Employer housing allowances in the UAE are
  typically paid only against a Tawtheeq-registered tenancy with the employee named as tenant
  [§2.3]. Every SPA-based "rent-to-own" in the market is therefore unfundable by that allowance
  [§2.2, RED rows]. A Tawtheeq-structured product is the only one that reaches the expat-with-
  allowance segment — a segment a brokerage sees daily and can size better than we can.
- **Fragmented ownership is their advantage, not their obstacle.** [INFERRED] If Al Reef and Hydra
  are landlord-by-landlord, PSI can canvass ten owners for one willing seller; a developer cannot.

### Why they might say no
- **They cannot deliver alone.** Any yes is contingent on finding a landlord, which pushes the real
  decision to Dossier 2. A PSI yes is procedural, not a product.
- **Deal economics for a broker are poor.** Standard agency commission is ~2% on a sale or ~5% of
  annual rent [§2.1, marked unverified]. A bespoke five-year structure with legal drafting, a
  purchase option and an equity ledger is disproportionate work for a commission on one unit.
- **Legal risk they may not want to carry.** A Tawtheeq tenancy carrying a purchase option is not a
  standard ADREC form. The research could not retrieve contract wording for *any* rent-to-own scheme
  in the emirate [§Gaps 1] — so there is no template anyone can point to. A broker may reasonably
  decline to be the party inventing one.
- **The Al Reem claim may be hollow.** If the article is search-term content rather than reportage,
  the conversation ends in the first ten minutes with "we don't actually have those."
- **Their book is apartments.** The only rent-to-own PSI referenced is Al Reem **apartments** — the
  wrong asset class for this brief [§1.4]. Villas and townhouses may simply not be their segment.

### Which desk, and via what channel
- **Channel:** website contact form, `psinv.net/en/contact-us` — **[UNCONFIRMED]**, from a search
  summary [Contacts]. **No phone number, no email address and no named individual for PSI surfaced

- **Desk:** ask for whoever handles **residential leasing / landlord representation**, not off-plan

- **Fallback route:** **Cresco Real Estate**, +971 52 102 1429 / info@crescorealestate.ae
  [Contacts — UNCONFIRMED], named in the research as the agent for Reportage Village. A second

### The one-sentence hook
> *"Your own 2026 article says rent-to-own is a practical route in Abu Dhabi — we have a funded
> tenant at AED 150,000 a year for a villa or townhouse and can find no such product in the market,
> so we are asking whether you can originate one."*

### What to ask for concretely
**A single unit, with one willing landlord.** Not a programme, not a term sheet with PSI.
1. A 45-minute call to establish whether the Al Reem claim is real and whether PSI has any landlord
   in Al Reef, Hydra Village, Khalifa City or Al Ghadeer open to a multi-year structure.

> "None of those developers (Manazel, Hydra Properties) were found to offer rent-to-own at all."

**[INFERRED] — the core commercial case, arithmetic shown**

Applying **Aldar's own published West Yas structure** (5-year term, 100% of rent converted to equity
at year 5) [§1.2] to outer-ring stock, using only research figures:

| | Al Reef (avg rent) | Al Reef (at the cap) | Hydra Village (mid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset price [KNOWN] | AED 1.10M | AED 1.10M | ~AED 0.83M (750–900k midpoint) |
| Annual rent [KNOWN] | AED 138,000 | AED 150,000 | AED 100,000 (top of Hydra range) |
| Gross yield | **12.5%** | **13.6%** | **12.0%** |
| Rent over 5 years | AED 690,000 | AED 750,000 | AED 500,000 |
| = % of asset value | **63%** | **68%** | **60%** |
| Residual/balloon at yr 5 | ~AED 410,000 | ~AED 350,000 | ~AED 330,000 |

Three consequences follow:

1. **The product is only financeable in this band.** Abu Dhabi's only ever villa rent-to-own was
   priced at **AED 220,000/yr plus AED 15,000/yr management** [§1.2] — the market has demonstrated
   villa rent-to-own starting ~50% above our cap. Outer-ring stock is the **only** inventory in the
   research where ≤150k/yr buys a genuinely large share of the asset.
2. **The landlord does not have to give away 100% of rent.** Aldar's Ansam/Al Hadeel/Meera scheme
   accrued equity at **~6–7% of property value per rent-year** [§1.3]. At Al Reef, rent is
   **12.5% of value per year**. A landlord could credit **half** the rent to equity, still beat
   Aldar's published accrual rate, and keep ~6% net yield as income. *This is the single strongest
   argument in the campaign and it belongs to this target and no other.*
3. **The residual is small enough to be conventional.** A ~AED 330–410k balloon on a ready freehold
   villa is an ordinary mortgage or Ijara size — ADIB indicative profit rates from ~3.75% p.a. (2026)
   [§A6]. Contrast West Yas, where the balloon was never disclosed at all [§Gaps 7] and the asset was
   far larger. **The exit is the thing that kills rent-to-own products, and here it is small.**

**[UNKNOWN] — and one of these is decisive**
- **Whether either company still owns any of this stock.** 2,376 freehold units at Al Reef, sold into
  a market since ~2012, are plausibly in private hands. If neither Manazel nor Hydra Properties holds
  inventory, **neither is a counterparty at all** and this dossier collapses into Dossier 1. *This is
  the first question to ask and it should be asked before any pitch.*
- Whether **Hydra Properties currently trades** as an active corporate entity. The research
  establishes Hydra Village as a community, not Hydra Properties as an operating company today.
- Whether either has a leasing/property-management arm, a legal function that could draft the option,
  or any appetite for multi-year commitments.
- Financial position, unsold inventory, occupancy, strategy, decision-makers — **all [UNKNOWN]**, and
  not guessed at.
- Whether the Al Reef/Hydra rent figures are current asking rents or historic.

### Why they might say yes
- **The economics work only here, and that is a fact about their stock, not about them.** They can be
  shown the table above and check it against their own numbers in five minutes.
- **No cannibalisation.** They have no rent-to-own product to undercut and no published price to
  contradict. Aldar cannot offer ≤150k without visibly repricing a 220k precedent; these landlords

- **Ready stock, immediate occupation** [§A7] — the product can start now, with no construction risk,
  no handover slippage and no off-plan escrow complexity.
- **Tawtheeq compliance costs almost nothing:** AED 900 one-off landlord property registration plus
  AED 50 per contract [§1.7]. The legal wrapper that makes this product fundable by an employer
  allowance costs under a thousand dirhams.
- **Cheap conversion at the end.** ADM/DARI transfer is **2% of price, reported split 1% buyer /
  1% seller** [§1.7] — a ~AED 11,000 seller-side cost on a 1.1M unit, against Dubai's 4%.
- **If the reported ADREC rent freeze is real, a fixed multi-year rent costs them nothing they were
  going to get anyway.** A temporary freeze on all rent increases effective 2 June 2026, halting the

  on adrec.gov.ae, treat as unverified.** Do not lead with this; if raised by them, it is a genuine

  **Do not overstate a claim resting on one blog.**

### Why they might say no
- **They may have nothing to sell.** See the decisive [UNKNOWN] above. This is the most likely
  reason the approach dies.
- **No product function.** A legacy developer of a community completed in 2012 may have no team that
  builds new commercial structures. "Nobody here does that" is a complete and sufficient no.
- **Aged inventory implies a discount motive, not a structuring motive.** [INFERRED] If they do hold
  units, the conventional response to slow stock is to cut price or offer an instalment plan — which

- **Five-year lock on a freehold unit at a fixed price forgoes upside.** They surrender any capital
  appreciation between now and year 5 for a yield they can largely get today by just letting the unit
  at 138k [§A7]. In a rising market this is a bad trade for them, and honestly so.
- **Counterparty risk sits with them.** A tenant who defaults in year 4 leaves the landlord with an
  equity ledger to unwind and a rental-dispute process to run. No template exists [§Gaps 1].
- **No contact route.** See below — this is a real obstacle, not a formality.

# SEQUENCING RECOMMENDATION

Order matters because this campaign is short of two things — **evidence** and **access** — and the
early calls should buy those rather than attempt a close.

### Wave 0 — before any counterparty is approached (week 1)
**Establish that the product is legally possible.**
1. **ADREC** — is a Tawtheeq tenancy with a purchase option and an equity ledger registrable on the
   standard form? Confirm the 2 June 2026 rent-freeze status while there [§Gaps 12].
2. **ADIB** — would they indicate willingness to finance a ~AED 330–410k residual on a ready Al Reef
   or Hydra Village villa at year 5?

*Why first:* both answers are **assets in every subsequent conversation**, and neither requires a
counterparty to commit anything. A landlord's most credible objections are "that's not registrable"
and "who takes out the balloon". **Arriving with both already answered converts the pitch from a
proposal into a plan.** Arriving without them means the first counterparty raises an objection we
cannot answer, and we lose the meeting for a reason that has nothing to do with them.

### Wave 1 — intelligence and access (weeks 1–3, run in parallel)
3. **PSI** [Dossier 1] — highest probability of a yes, lowest cost of a no, and the only party who
   might tell us **who actually owns lettable Al Reef and Hydra Village stock**. That answer resolves
   the decisive [UNKNOWN] in Dossier 2.
4. **Cresco Real Estate** — second aggregator; simultaneously settles the Reportage price conflict
   [§Gaps 10] without spending the Reportage approach.
5. **Aldar Estates** [Dossier 3] — approach **before** Aldar Properties. Lowest operational lift of
   any corporate target, and the party most likely to answer the Tawtheeq-vs-SPA question on West Yas
   [§Gaps 1] — the single most important unanswered question in the research. An internal referral
   from Estates into the group is also worth more than a cold customer-care ticket at Properties.
6. **Modon** [Who is missing] — cold, contact details to be found. The only party with demonstrated
   public Tawtheeq expertise. Ask about structure, not about Al Naseem.

### Wave 2 — the real close (weeks 3–8)
7. **Manazel / Al Reef and Hydra Properties** [Dossier 2] — armed with Wave 0's registrability and
   residual answers, and Wave 1's ownership intelligence.

*Why not first, despite ranking 2nd on probability:* **we currently have no contact route to either
company.** Approaching them before Wave 1 means a cold enquiry into an unknown switchboard with no
regulatory backing and no financing partner. Approaching them after means arriving through a broker
who knows the stock, with a registrable structure and a named residual financier. **The same pitch,
delivered eight weeks later, is a materially better pitch.** Sequencing is doing most of the work here.

### Wave 3 — precedent-dependent (weeks 8+)
8. **Aldar Properties** [Dossier 4].
9. **Bloom Holding** [Dossier 5].
10. **Reportage Properties** [Dossier 6] — **only if** the AED 1.35M figure survives Cresco.
11. **Q Properties / Q Holding**, then IMKAN, Eagle Hills, Arada, Nine Yards — cold, low expectation.

### The three ordering principles behind this

**a) A small yes is citable; a large no is not costly.**
A signed pilot on three Al Reef units — even at one landlord, even informally — converts every later
pitch from "would you invent this" to "this exists in Abu Dhabi and you are not in it." That is why
the outer-ring landlords must be closed **before** Aldar is asked to reprice, not after. **Aldar
cannot be persuaded by an argument; it can be moved by a competitor's precedent.**

**b) A no from Aldar costs almost nothing — but a no from Aldar *first* costs the campaign a story.**
Approaching Aldar early and being refused hands every other target the line "even Aldar passed."
Approaching Aldar late, holding a live pilot, inverts it. The asymmetry is entirely in the ordering,
not in the ask.

**c) Buy answers before asking for commitments.**
The research leaves the campaign's central question — Tawtheeq or SPA — unanswered for **every scheme
in the emirate** [§Gaps 1]. Until that is resolved, we are asking counterparties to commit to a
structure whose legal form we cannot describe. Waves 0 and 1 exist to fix that. **Do not spend the
Manazel or Aldar approach before it is fixed; those are the two approaches worth protecting.**

### Honest overall read — **judgement, not data**
Probability that any counterparty on this list creates a compliant ≤150k/yr Tawtheeq-registered
villa/townhouse rent-to-own product within 12 months: **roughly 10–15%.**
Probability that a **single bespoke unit** is transacted on those terms — one landlord, one tenant,
one contract, no programme: **materially higher, perhaps 25–30%**, and almost certainly through
Dossier 1 or Dossier 2 rather than through any developer.
**The campaign's realistic best outcome is one transaction that becomes a precedent, not a product
launch.** It should be sequenced for that outcome.
